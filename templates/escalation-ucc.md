# Escalating an unresolved complaint

If your provider hasn't fixed the problem within a reasonable time, you can take
it further. Always complain to the provider **first**: regulators usually ask
for your provider complaint reference number.

## Who handles it

The **Uganda Communications Commission (UCC)** regulates communications services
in Uganda, including consumer complaints about quality of service and radio
interference. Check UCC's official website for its current complaint channels
(online form, phone, email or office visit). Contact details change, so Kebera
does not list them here.

## What to include

- Your name, phone number and area
- Your provider, service type (5G/4G home internet, fibre, …) and account/line number
- Your provider complaint **reference number(s)**, dates, and what they told you
- A short summary of the problem and how long it has lasted
- Evidence: your Kebera report(s) (`report.md` / `report.html`) and long-log
  report(s), ideally from different days and times

## Example summary

> Since [date], my [provider] [5G home internet] has had frequent drops and severe
> lag. Diagnostic tests (attached) show my own equipment is healthy and the loss
> happens in the provider network: [x]% loss, delay spikes up to [y] ms, and
> [n] outages during a [h]-hour log on [date]. I reported this to [provider] on
> [date] (reference [number]) and [what happened]. I request that UCC follow up
> with [provider] to resolve the quality of service problem.

## Keep it factual

Stick to what you measured and what the provider said. Kebera reports are
evidence of what your connection did during the tests, not proof of the
provider's intent or of a contract breach.
