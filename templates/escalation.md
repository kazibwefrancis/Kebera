# Escalating with your service provider

If your first complaint doesn't get the problem fixed, move **up the levels inside
your provider**, one step at a time. Every step uses the same evidence: your Kebera
report and your reference number(s).

## Before you start

Keep a simple record (a note on your phone is fine):

| Date and time | Channel (WhatsApp, call, shop…) | Agent's name | Reference number | What they said or promised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Always ask for a **reference number** and an **expected time to fix**. If they say
"we'll call you back", ask "by when?" and note it.

## The escalation ladder

### Level 1: Normal customer care

WhatsApp, the call centre or the provider's app. Send your
`message-to-provider.txt` or the [WhatsApp template](message-whatsapp.md).

- Ask for: a network check of your area, whether your line is limited, and a reference number.
- Give it the time they promise (often 24–72 hours).

### Level 2: Follow up and ask for the technical team

If nothing changes by the promised time, reply in the **same** conversation:

> Hello, following up on reference [number] from [date]. The problem continues. My new
> test today ([date/time]) shows [x]% loss and delays up to [y] ms beyond my equipment.
> Please escalate this to the **technical / network team** and confirm a date for a fix
> or a technician visit.

Attach your newest Kebera report (`report.html` or `report.md`) or screenshots of it.

### Level 3: Supervisor or escalation desk

If the second promise passes too, ask for a **supervisor**, a **team leader** or the
**complaints/escalations desk**:

> I've reported this [n] times (references [list]) since [date]. I'd like this handled by
> a supervisor or the escalations team. I can share detailed test results showing the
> problem is on the network side.

### Level 4: Service centre visit

Visit a provider **service centre or shop** with:

- Your Kebera report(s), printed or on your phone
- Your reference numbers and your record table
- Your equipment, if they may need to test or swap it (outdoor unit, router)

Ask for a technician visit, a unit swap, or a formal written escalation.

### Level 5: Formal written complaint

Send a formal complaint by **email** (or letter) to the provider's official customer
care or complaints address, listed on their website or SIM pack. Use the
[email template](message-email.md) and add:

- A short history: dates, channels, reference numbers, what each promised
- Your **detailed report** ([report template](diagnosis-report.md)) and Kebera reports
- What you want: a fix by a date, a technician visit, equipment replacement, or a
  credit or refund for days without proper service, if your plan allows it

Example opening:

> Formal complaint: unresolved service problem on line [number], first reported [date]
> (references [list]). Despite [n] contacts, the problem continues. Detailed test results
> are attached. I request [what you want] by [date].

## Tips that help

- **Stay polite and factual.** Agents respond better to clear evidence than to anger.
- **Test again before each follow-up**, ideally at the bad time (e.g. evening). A fresh
  report shows the problem hasn't gone away.
- **Use the same conversation thread** so the history stays together.
- **Keep it about evidence**: "my equipment tests healthy; the loss happens beyond it."
- **Leave out private details**: no passwords; share your account number only through
  official channels.
